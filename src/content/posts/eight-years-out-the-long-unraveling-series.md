Eight Years Out: The Long Unraveling

​Canvas I: Fresh Ground
Eight years away from the burn, and life is finally growing sweet in the places they said were ruined.

The soil remembers how to bloom once you stop watering it with ash.

​Canvas II: Unclenching
A slammed door is just noise now; my body has stopped waiting for a fight every time the phone lights up.


A heavy door is only wood and hinges now; the pulse stays slow, the ghost stays quiet.

​Canvas III: Dropping the Tab
I woke up one day and realized I stopped calculating everything they stole from my younger self.

The stolen years drift off like smoke uncounted, unpaid, and no longer mine to carry.

​Canvas IV: Safe Hands
Learning to let good people close without bracing for the catch.

Soft light enters through open doors, no longer mistaken for a weapon in disguise.

​Canvas V: My Own Space
Owning my time, my peace, and my silence without feeling like I have to apologize for existing.

I reign over silence, owing no tax on the air I take into my lungs.

​Canvas VI: Nothing Left to Burn
No anger, no missing them, just looking back at their house like a stranger passing by.

I turn my eyes back toward the distant fire and feel only the cool, evening air on my face.

​At year eight, my healing stops feeling like an emergency and starts feeling like home. My nervous system relaxes, the scorekeeping fades, and I trade the obsession with being understood for the quiet joy of being free.
